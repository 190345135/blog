AI-Automated OSINT: Intelligence Profiling from Leaked Data at Scale

DECEMBER 2024 — OSINT / AI AUTOMATION / THREAT INTELLIGENCE

While reviewing the United States Secret Service's Most Wanted list, I came across an interesting case: Denis Gennadievich Kulkov, known in underground forums as "Nordex", "Nordexin", and "Kreenjo" — the operator behind Try2check, a bulk stolen credit card validation service. The USSS had posted a $10 million reward for information leading to his capture.

I decided to use this as a test case for an automated OSINT methodology I had been developing.

The Problem

Manually searching a target's aliases across multiple leaked databases, stealer logs, and public records is effective but painfully slow. A single investigation can take an analyst days of cross-referencing across fragmented data sources. Each alias needs to be checked against dozens of breached datasets, correlated with other identities, and validated for accuracy.

The question was straightforward: could an AI agent replicate an experienced analyst's workflow and compress days of manual work into minutes?

Data Sources

Specialized services like IntelX provide access to indexed stealer logs and breached databases, but access costs upward of $10,000. However, equivalent raw data is freely accessible through OSINT collection — Telegram groups distributing credential dumps (Joghood Team, Watercloud Logs, Daisy Cloud, Satanic Cloud, among others), publicly indexed breach repositories, and stealer log aggregators.

No exploitation is involved in this methodology. This is pure collection and indexing of already-public material — the legal and ethical implications depend entirely on how the resulting intelligence is applied.

The Pipeline

I built a pipeline integrating an AI agent with custom search tools I had developed for querying indexed breach data and stealer logs. The agent received a single directive: "Conduct a complete OSINT investigation on Denis Gennadievich Kulkov and his known aliases."

Working autonomously, the agent executed the following workflow:

  1. Cross-referenced all known aliases across multiple breached databases
  2. Correlated overlapping records between different data sources to identify linked identities
  3. Extracted metadata associated with matched accounts — registration dates, IP addresses, device fingerprints
  4. Identified behavioral patterns and digital footprint signatures across platforms
  5. Mapped infrastructure elements connected to the Try2check operation

Results

From nothing more than a name and a set of usernames — all publicly available on the USSS wanted poster — the automated pipeline produced:

  • Social security number
  • Registered vehicles including license plates and VIN numbers
  • Probable family members identified through cross-source data correlation
  • Precise geographic locations extracted from GPS metadata embedded in delivery application accounts linked to compromised credentials

Implications

This demonstrates the force-multiplying effect of AI-driven OSINT. The same investigation that would take an analyst days of manual work was completed in minutes with comparable accuracy. The agent does not replace human judgment — it handles the mechanical correlation at scale, freeing the analyst to focus on interpretation, validation, and action.

The methodology applies equally to threat intelligence, fraud investigation, and red team reconnaissance. When a target's digital footprint spans dozens of breached databases and stealer log repositories, manual correlation is not just slow — it is incomplete. An analyst working by hand will inevitably miss connections that automated cross-referencing catches.
